Now transformed into a cultural arts center, the Workhouse complex at Lorton
started out as a prison farm for nonviolent offenders accused of crimes like
vagrancy or drunkenness. The penitentiary buildings were constructed by the
prisoners themselves, using bricks manufactured at the on-site kiln complex.
Fairfax County purchased the facility in 2002.
Guard towers were added in the 1930s as the prison grew overcrowded. What
began in the early 1900s as a self-sufficient industrial farm for petty criminals
later added a high-security penitentiary for those convicted of rape, murder, and
armed robbery.
