By the late 1930s, a formal neoclassical garden took shape on the Laural hillside — a fountain, a retaining pool, and terraced brick walks, believed to have been built by incarcerated laborers as a way to teach construction and gardening skills. Volunteer clean-up days through the 1980s and '90s (including a 1997 Eagle Scout project) kept it going, but today the garden is almost entirely reclaimed by vegetation.
